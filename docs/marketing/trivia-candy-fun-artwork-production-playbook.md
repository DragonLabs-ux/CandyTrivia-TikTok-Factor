# Trivia Candy Fun Artwork Production Playbook

Version 1.0 · Prepared September 30, 2026

## Purpose

A repeatable process for creating polished campaign artwork that feels like the live Trivia Candy Fun experience and clearly directs people to either the web game or the iOS app. Use this playbook for social posts, short-form video covers, web banners, and promotional campaign art.

## Brand and product references

Check these sources before each campaign because product features and artwork can change:

- [Trivia Candy Fun web edition](https://dragonlabs.app/arcade/trivia-candy-fun/)
- [Trivia Candy Fun on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/trivia-candy-fun/id6768475077)
- [Apple App Store marketing and identity guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/marketing/guidelines/)
- Internal visual direction: Trivia Candy Fun Web Arcade Handover, September 28, 2026

### What the current visual identity looks like

The live website and App Store screens point to one coherent visual world:

- Candy Kingdom fantasy setting: glowing candy castle, pink and violet skies, candy paths, oversized sweets, clouds, sparkles, and friendly in-game candy characters.
- Rich grape and royal purple anchor colors, with candy pink and magenta, warm cream panels, gold trim, and occasional rainbow accents.
- Glossy, dimensional candy surfaces and polished game buttons; soft glow and highlights should add depth without making the scene hazy.
- Rounded panels, clear visual hierarchy, bold playful headlines, and readable supporting text.
- Composition should feel bright, magical, and premium, not babyish or cluttered.

Use the exact logo, app icon, character art, UI screenshots, and color/type tokens from the current project files when available. Treat the live screenshots as visual references, not permission to redraw product interfaces. Do not guess exact color codes from a screenshot if the source design tokens are available.

## Keep the destination clear

Each creative should have one main action. Do not mix web-play claims with app-only features.

| Campaign destination | Safe message direction | Main call to action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Web edition | Five-question free rounds, no timer, choose a category, no sign-in required for free rounds | Play the web edition: https://dragonlabs.app/arcade/trivia-candy-fun/ |
| iOS app | Candy trivia challenges, Global Daily Challenge, Mystery Wheel, Quick Play, category play, and supported friend/rival play as shown in the current listing | Get Trivia Candy Fun on the App Store: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/trivia-candy-fun/id6768475077 |

Useful current copy from the live site includes “A little trivia. A lot of sweet.” and “Choose your world.” The App Store subtitle is “Sweet trivia, candy challenges.”

### Claims to check before publishing

The live website currently says 32 categories and 7 premium categories. The App Store listing currently says 24 categories and 10,000+ questions. Those counts conflict, so leave category and question counts out of promotional artwork and captions until the product owner confirms a single current number.

The App Store listing shows a 13+ age rating. Keep the tone playful, but do not describe the app as made for young children or target children without a separate age and policy review.

Do not imply that the web version includes features that are only described for the iOS app, or vice versa. Avoid changing prices, purchase terms, ratings, or performance claims unless they have been checked against the current official source immediately before use.

## Repeatable production workflow

### 1. Write a one-card creative brief

Before generating art, fill in:

- Campaign goal: web plays, App Store visits, feature awareness, or trivia engagement.
- Audience: trivia and casual-game fans; use the product's current rating and policies when targeting.
- One hook: a question, a candy clue, or one current feature.
- Destination and exact URL.
- Placement and aspect ratio.
- Required product assets: logo, app icon, approved character art, or current gameplay screenshot.
- Exact copy and any factual claim source.

Choose one concept per asset. Examples: “Can you name all three candies?”, “Spin for today's topic,” or “Take today's trivia challenge.”

### 2. Gather current reference material

Collect the current website hero image and current App Store screenshots. Use approved original product files for the logo, icon, characters, and interface. If none are available, use the current page screenshots as references and keep AI-generated interface elements out of the final design.

For web-game creative, show web-game visuals and link to the web edition. For app-download creative, show current iPhone/iPad gameplay and link directly to the App Store product page.

### 3. Generate the scene, not the final text

Use an image-generation tool with the website and App Store art as references. Ask it to create the background/key art, reserve clean space for copy, and avoid lettering. AI-generated text, badges, logos, and detailed UI are unreliable; add those elements later from approved files in a design editor.

Generate 2–4 compositions for one concept. Pick the clearest one and refine its crop, lighting, and negative space before adding copy.

### 4. Add approved brand assets and copy

Place the official logo or app icon without changing its proportions or lettering. Add a short hook, no more than one supporting line, and one main CTA. Use real screenshots for interface previews; do not ask the image model to invent screens.

When using an App Store badge, download the official badge artwork. Apple prefers the black badge; its white alternative may suit a dark composition. Use one badge per layout or video, keep it subordinate to the main message, and do not modify, rotate, or animate it. Link the badge to the exact product page above.

### 5. Adapt for the placement

- TikTok/Reels/Shorts: design vertical 9:16; keep key copy and the product mark clear of platform controls and captions.
- Feed image: make a separate square or 4:5 composition instead of shrinking the vertical version.
- Website banner: use a wide layout with a clear playable-game or App Store CTA.
- App Store screenshots: use the current required device sizes and Apple's current screenshot guidance. Do not reuse social-media exports as App Store screenshots without checking those requirements.

Preview every crop at its final size. If a headline, app icon, CTA, or game clue becomes hard to read, simplify and recompose.

### 6. Write the caption and destination link

Caption pattern:

Hook/question + one accurate product benefit + one CTA + destination URL + a few relevant hashtags.

Example for web play:
“Can you go 3 for 3? Try a quick candy quiz—no timer. Play the web edition: https://dragonlabs.app/arcade/trivia-candy-fun/ #Trivia #CandyTrivia #Quiz”

Example for the iOS app:
“Last clue is a classic—did you get it? Get Trivia Candy Fun on the App Store: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/trivia-candy-fun/id6768475077 #Trivia #CandyTok #QuizTok #TriviaCandyFun”

Use 3–5 relevant hashtags. Do not place an App Store CTA on a creative whose destination is the web game unless it is clearly secondary.

### 7. Quality review, export, and archive

Review the full-size source and each platform crop side by side with the current website or App Store reference. Use the gates below before export. Keep the prompt, source references, final copy, destination URL, review date, and final exports together so the next campaign can repeat or improve the process.

## Reusable art prompt

Use this as a starting prompt, then replace the bracketed details:

> Create polished promotional key art for Trivia Candy Fun using the supplied live website screenshot and current App Store screenshots as visual references. Match the established Candy Kingdom: glowing candy castle, pink-violet fantasy sky, colorful candy paths, oversized glossy sweets, gentle sparkle highlights, friendly approved game characters where supplied, rich purple and magenta, warm cream framing, and refined gold accents. Make it feel like premium, playful trivia-game artwork with clean shapes, strong contrast, dimensional candy surfaces, and a clear focal point. Compose for [placement and aspect ratio]. Show [one approved story or feature]. Leave uncluttered negative space at [copy placement] for the real title and CTA to be added later. Keep important visual elements inside the platform-safe area. Do not add words, letters, logos, app icons, fake screenshots, invented controls, badges, prices, category counts, or unsupported feature claims. Do not imitate another Dragon Labs app or use unapproved branded candy packaging.

For app-specific layouts, place current in-game screenshots into the design after image generation. For web-play layouts, use the current web-edition artwork and the web CTA.

## Final approval checklist

Score each line from 0 to 2 (0 = fail, 1 = needs revision, 2 = ready):

- Brand match: unmistakably Candy Kingdom, polished purple/cream/gold/candy direction.
- Product accuracy: real approved logo, characters, screenshots, and verified claims.
- Destination clarity: one CTA; web play and App Store routes are not confused.
- Crop and legibility: readable at phone size, key content avoids interface overlays.
- Craft: clean edges, strong hierarchy, balanced lighting, no visual artifacts.

Ready-to-export score: at least 9/10, with no critical failure. Critical failures include wrong app/account, incorrect destination, invented product UI or claims, unreadable CTA, altered Apple badge, or a style that belongs to a different Dragon Labs app.

## File naming and handoff

Use a stable filename pattern:

trivia-candy-fun_[campaign]_[placement]_[ratio]_v01_YYYY-MM-DD

For each export, record:

- Final PNG/MP4 and editable source file.
- Destination: web edition or App Store.
- Caption and exact URL.
- Source screenshots/assets and their review date.
- Generation prompt and any editor adjustments.
- Reviewer and quality score.
